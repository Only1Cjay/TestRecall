How to use TestRecall

  1. Open the app and install it if you want it on your home screen: menu → Install app (Android), or Safari Share → Add to Home Screen (iPhone).
  2. Pick a course on the home screen (PHM 201, PCG, PCH 201, PCT, PIO), then tap a set. Use the search bar to find a set by name.
  3. Choose how to practise: Exam simulation — answer everything first, score and review at the end, like the real thing. Instant marking — each answer is marked the moment you tap it, and the correct answer is shown. Then set a timer in minutes, or tap Untimed.
  4. Answer the questions: tap an option to answer. Tap the flag to mark a question. Tap the middle grid button (e.g. 24/122) to jump to any question. Use Prev / Next to move. Tap Submit answers when you're done. If the timer runs out, it submits itself.
  5. See your results: your score, a heatmap of every question, and filters for All / Failed / Correct / Flagged. Tap any question to review it. Then Retake the set or Practise missed only.
  6. Review everything you missed: the blue card on the home screen builds one mixed session from every question you missed across all your sets (or inside a course, just that course's missed questions).
  7. Add your own sets: tap the + button (bottom right) and upload an MCQ PDF, or paste the questions as text. Pick the course it belongs to, or create a new one. The app reads the PDF on your phone.
  8. Delete a set: tap the trash icon on a set card and confirm. The built-in starter sets only hide on your own phone; bring them back in Settings → Restore starter sets.
  9. Create a course: tap the circular + at the top right of the home screen, enter the course code (e.g. PCH 201) and the full name (e.g. Pharmaceutical Chemistry). A course with no sets stays hidden until you add one.
  10. Streaks: every set you submit counts for that day.
  11. Offline: after the first time you open the app online, everything works offline, including uploading and reading PDFs.

Making your own MCQ sets

Use this prompt with any AI when you upload your lecture notes or textbook pages, so the questions come out in the exact format TestRecall accepts.

Create as many exam-style multiple choice questions as you can from my uploaded material. Follow this strict format exactly:
1. Number the questions in order, one per line: '1. Question text?'
2. Each question has exactly four options, one per line: 'A. ...', 'B. ...', 'C. ...', 'D. ...'
3. Exactly one correct answer per question.
4. Do not write 'A.', 'B.', 'C.' or 'D.' anywhere inside a question or option text.
5. Do not use section headings. Mix the questions fully, like a real exam: do not follow the order of the source material, and do not cluster questions on the same topic or example together.
6. Spread the correct answers evenly across A, B, C and D, with no pattern, and do not make the correct option longer or shorter than the others.
7. Test examples and applications, not just definitions.
8. At the very end, under the heading 'Answer Key', give the key in rows of 10: one line with the question numbers, then one line with the matching letters. Nothing after the key.
